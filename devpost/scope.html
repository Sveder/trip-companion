Trip Companion (working title)

A kid-friendly, adult-friendly app that gets a family ready and excited for a trip abroad. Canonical file: devpost/scope.md.

The kernel. Not one feature: “a lot of good ideas coming together into one package that is very accessible” — for children and adults — that prepares you so “you want to be excited about it”. Excitement first; knowledge comes with it.

Who it's for

A parent planning a trip abroad with small children — here Michael, with a 4-year-old and a 2-year-old — and the children themselves. Today the parent reads guide books alone, and the kids arrive knowing nothing about the place.

The core loop

Open the app (TV, phone or laptop) → do today's short activity together → a quiz that repeats earlier words and facts → come back tomorrow for the next step.

Identity

Bright and game-like, like Matific. Place facts in the spirit of Geography Now. Mood: “ready and pumped”.

The boundary

Now — in the proof of concept
  • Japan only, in full, with “quite a lot of activities” (the list is decided in the PRD).
  • Other countries, Uzbekistan first, as “coming soon” / beta-preview cards.
  • The builder: who travels (adults, children and ages), how many days → a course of daily steps.
  • Japan modules: food (watchable on a TV), words with repetition, do and don't in public, safety (emergency numbers).
  • Real-world guided activities, e.g. chopsticks with a real pair.
  • A quiz that repeats earlier items.
  • Transport: how to get around (trains, buses).
  • A guided Street View tour of places you will visit.
  • A buy-in-advance list, e.g. practice chopsticks.
  • After the trip: highlights.
  • Kid-friendly UI on a phone and in the TV's web browser.
Later — kept, not now
  • More modules: what to wear, curated videos.
  • Shop or affiliate links on the buy-in-advance list.
  • A real LG webOS app (sideloaded) instead of the TV browser.
  • Full content for more countries, Uzbekistan first.
Cut — with a reason
  • Nothing cut yet. Every idea is in “now” or “later”.

Why this matters

A real family trip to Japan with two small boys. And: “to understand if a plan first approach is going to be good and interesting.”
